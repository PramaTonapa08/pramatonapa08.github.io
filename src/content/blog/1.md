Walking onto the Cenderawasih University campus in Jayapura for the first time feels a lot like looking at an uncleaned dataset: overwhelming, full of unfamiliar variables, and slightly intimidating. As a first-semester Statistics student, I quickly realized that university life isn't just about memorizing formulas—it's about learning how to find patterns in the chaos.

Between morning lectures overlooking the hills of Waena and late-night study sessions trying to make sense of foundational calculus and probability theory, the transition from high school is steep. You start seeing the world differently. Suddenly, waiting for a *angkot* isn't just an annoyance; it's a real-world exercise in queuing theory and arrival distributions.

## The shift from numbers to logic

In high school, math is mostly about finding the single right answer. In college-level statistics, the focus shifts to understanding *why* a distribution behaves the way it does, and how to defend your assumptions when data gets messy. 

The early weeks are packed with foundational courses—introductory statistics, linear algebra, and basic programming. It can feel abstract at first, especially when you are debugging your very first lines of R or Python while your peers are already talking about data visualization.

## Finding rhythm in Jayapura

Life at Uncen has its own unique pace. Between classes at FMIPA, grabbing quick lunches near campus, and getting caught in sudden afternoon tropical downpours, you learn to adapt. Group study sessions become essential—not just for surviving problem sets, but for realizing that everyone else is figuring things out one step at a time too.

Semester one is really just an exploratory data analysis of your own potential. There is plenty of noise, a few outliers, and a lot of trial and error—but slowly, the trendline starts pointing upward.
